# 0x0sky

**Software engineer · systems architect · poet**

Kyiv, Ukraine · [mind.nilx.one](https://mind.nilx.one)

> I build systems that remain clear when the world becomes complicated.

Most software problems are not code problems. They begin earlier — as blurred intent, weak boundaries, or assumptions that were never turned into contracts.

I work across that entire distance: from the first idea to the architecture, from the architecture to the implementation, and from the implementation to a system a person can actually trust.

My work lives where product, infrastructure, protocols, automation, and AI meet. I care about software that is legible under pressure, provider-agnostic by design, secure at its boundaries, and honest about what it can do.

Built from Kyiv, where reliability is not a slogan and reality reaches production before the roadmap does.

## operating principles

- contracts over assumptions
- predictability over cleverness
- architecture before implementation
- explicit boundaries over invisible coupling
- security enforced in code, not promised in slides
- aesthetics without loss of purpose
- movement without haste

## engineering language

**Swift** is how I shape public surfaces — safe, readable, and intentional.

**Ruby** is how I think about private motion — expressive, flexible, and close to the problem.

**C** is the instrument underneath the instrument — structures, types, memory, algorithms.

**Rust** and **Go** enter where infrastructure, portability, and hard boundaries matter.

**AI/ML** is leverage, not theater.

**Poetry** is compression without the loss of meaning.

These are not isolated projects. They are parts of one direction: software that can explain itself, infrastructure that does not require ritual, and tools that increase human agency without hiding their own behavior.

## what I am building toward

I believe identity should be portable.

I believe infrastructure should be understandable from first principles.

I believe AI systems should operate through explicit permissions, observable actions, and reversible decisions.

I believe protocols can describe more than machines — they can describe trust, connection, consent, and coordinated action without reducing people to data.

And I believe the best product architecture feels less like machinery and more like inevitability: every part has a reason to exist, and nothing needs to shout.
